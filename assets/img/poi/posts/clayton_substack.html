EBC & [POI] Profiles: Jay Clayton. Second in a series on the people we're watching. He gets a profile page on everyballotcounts.io that keeps updating after this post goes stale.

Last Tuesday, in the early morning hours, from Manila, the White House chief of staff took the microphone away from the Secretary of Homeland Security and handed it to somebody else. A person close to the White House explained: "We need to speak with one voice and the right voice, and the decision was made to put Jay out there."1

Jay is Jay Clayton, the Director of National Intelligence. This morning he was out there on ABC, CNN and Fox, all before lunch.2 (ABC's own transcript calls him the "Director of National Security." Honestly? Give it a month.)

So who is the right voice, and why should you care what it says?

Because in about four weeks, this is the man who owes the President a report on whether foreigners interfered in the election Democrats just won.3 If you've read this series, you know that report is step one of the worst case. And the guy who writes it is now also the guy explaining the hacks.

The résumé is long. One section is short.

Clayton is a deal lawyer, and a very good one. By his own Senate paperwork: Sullivan & Cromwell partner. Chairman of the SEC, 2017 to 2020. Then chair of Apollo Global Management, a seat on the American Express board, and a string of advisory gigs at hedge funds and crypto firms.4

In April 2025 Trump made him the top federal prosecutor in Manhattan on an interim basis, which got around Sen. Chuck Schumer's promise to block him. Judges kept him in the job that August.5

What's not on the list: a single day working in intelligence. The best his paperwork could offer was a unit in his prosecutor's office that "interacts with various U.S. intelligence and counterterrorism personnel daily."6 A former chair of the National Intelligence Council put it more plainly: "he comes into the job without much experience, either in national security or, more specifically, intelligence."7

He got the job anyway, for a very Trump reason. The first choice to run things was Bill Pulte, the housing regulator, and Congress revolted. Clayton was the name that made the revolt stop.8 He was confirmed July 28.9

He was the adult. That was the entire pitch. So what did the adult say to get hired?

The job interview

Three things, and all three are about elections.

One. A month before his hearing, on CNBC, talking about how long California takes to count: "On the integrity side, we're doing an absolutely terrible job."10 He also called mail ballots an "opportunity for fraud."

Two. At the hearing, Sen. Jon Ossoff asked him who won in 2020. Clayton would say Biden was "certified." He would not say Biden won. "I think I've answered the question. We can keep doing this." Also: "I'm not an election denier."11

Three. Senators asked about his predecessor showing up at the FBI's raid on a Georgia election office. He said he'd first heard about it from Ossoff the day before. Then: "To be clear, the ODNI's role is principally outside of the United States."

Hold on to that one. Because in his written answers to the same committee, he said the DNI "has substantial statutory authority to address national intelligence threats to U.S. elections."12

Principally outside the United States ... with substantial authority over U.S. elections. A good lawyer can stand in both places at once. He's a very good lawyer.

(In fairness: Rep. Jim Himes, the top Democrat on House Intelligence, says that when Clayton took over, "we asked and received the required classified summary of influence and interference." The legally required pre-election reports were still missing in late October.13)

Which brings us to this morning.

Sunday, read like a deposition

Here's the thing about careful lawyers. They don't lie. They scope. Every answer has an edge, and the interesting part is what's on the other side of it.

The outages (flights, banks, Microsoft, the NSA). Flat denial, same as he gave Congress on Wednesday:14

"The incidents that we saw during the course of this week, we know that they were not coordinated and that they were not state sponsored."

The hacks. This week also brought two actual break-ins: the FBI's national crime database, and the Agriculture Department's system for paying farmers.15 Nobody's calling those a glitch. Jonathan Karl asked who did it. Twice.

"They have an incident. They're dealing with it in the ordinary course."

"Again, not getting into specifics."

On Fox: "They are going to find out the origin, the scope, and the like, and then we will communicate to the American people."16

Read those answers side by side. The outages get "not coordinated, not state sponsored." The hacks get nothing. No denial, no country, no "we've looked very closely." The blank from the FBI's alert is still blank, and the person holding the pen just told three networks he isn't ready to fill it in. [P]

(Regular readers just twitched at "ordinary course." Different statute. Relax. For now.)

The promise. Same interview. Same chair. I am not making this up:

"... transparency is key. We gather the facts and then we share them with the American people."

"Not getting into specifics" and "transparency is key," back to back. Even I laughed.

Then I remembered that nothing in the order behind the Dec. 18 report requires anyone to share a word of it with the American people. It goes to the President, who heard about the FBI hack and said maybe we'll learn about "all the voter fraud that took place."17

The forecast. On ABC: "we're going to see more of that." On CNN: "We are going to have glitches, gaps, interruptions in service. That is going to happen." And: "We are taking more systems down and putting them back up than ever before."18

So the outages aren't over. They're scheduled.

And then Karl asked him about a different day.

"You can call it what you want"

The nominee to run the government's cybersecurity agency told senators this week that the Capitol was not attacked on Jan. 6. So Karl asked the Director of National Intelligence the same question: was there an attack on the Capitol?

"I'm not going to relitigate that issue here. I'm moving forward."

Karl: I'm just asking if it happened. The answer: the American people saw it, and "it was a very unfortunate day for America."

Karl tried again. And again. And again.

"I'm not going to get into characterizations."

"You can call it what you want. Every American can call it what you want. There was clearly violence."

And finally: "there was clearly trespass."

Trespass.

I want to be precise about why this matters, because it isn't about Jan. 6. This man's whole job on Dec. 18 is characterization. Was it "interference"? Was a system "accessed"? Was it "foreign-linked"? The law that follows turns on which word he picks.

And this morning he showed us how he picks. The thing on video with the broken windows is a "characterization" he won't get into. The outages are "not state sponsored," full stop. The two hacks are an "incident" in the "ordinary course," origin to be announced.

He'll name it when it's useful to name. [P]

One more thing they wrote down

While I've got you. The first Trump White House put this in its 2018 cyber strategy:

"In the event of a significant cyber incident, the Federal Government is poised to provide threat and asset response to recover election infrastructure."19

"Threat response" is the FBI. "Asset response" is CISA. "Election infrastructure" belongs to your state and your county. They wrote that step down eight years ago. This year's strategy doesn't mention it ... which is a post of its own, and it's coming.

What I'm watching

  • Any sentence from Clayton that puts voter or citizenship data inside a breached system.

  • Any country. For either hack.

  • Whether "the outages" quietly becomes "the incidents."

  • Dec. 18, and whether we get the report, a summary, or a Truth Social post.

  • Whether he keeps doing the DHS Secretary's talking. (The one who signs the declaration is still on time-out.)

The right voice was calm this morning. It was smooth, it was lawyerly, and it said more outages are coming and we'll be told who hacked the FBI when there's something to tell.

I believe the first part.

Hi, Jay. 👋
