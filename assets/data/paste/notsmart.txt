<h2>Not part of what, now?</h2>
<p>The day after a single router in El Dorado, Arkansas, flooded the network and grounded every departure in the country, FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford wanted us to keep two things apart: "We're conflating modernization with this upgrade at this particular facility. ... This is not part of the Peraton-FAA $12.5 billion modernization effort."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-1" href="#footnote-1">1</a></p>
<p>So I looked up El Dorado on the FAA's own modernization website. You can too. Type it into the "Local Impact" box. Here's what it says, as of today:</p>
<p><strong>"TDM-TO-IP / TIR — PHASE 1 — IN PROGRESS."</strong><a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-2" href="#footnote-2">2</a></p>
<p><img src="https://everyballotcounts.io/assets/img/poi/posts/notsmart_quote_square_1080.png" alt="FAA Administrator, Nov. 14: &quot;This is not part of the Peraton-FAA $12.5 billion modernization effort.&quot; The FAA's own website, for El Dorado, Ark., where the router went in: TDM-TO-IP / TIR, Phase 1, In progress. Comment [A1]: Narrator: it was."></p>
<p>That's the copper-to-fiber conversion. Listed as Phase 1 of the program Bedford says it isn't part of. On his agency's website. With the little blue "in progress" tag and everything.</p>
<p>That set the tone for the whole week. Every official explanation of the ground stop comes with a "not." So let's take them one at a time and hold each one up against the government's own paperwork.</p>
<h2>The cast</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Sean Duffy</strong>, Transportation Secretary. Wants $50 billion more.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-3" href="#footnote-3">3</a></li>
<li><strong>Bryan Bedford</strong>, FAA Administrator. Signed the waiver you'll meet below.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-4" href="#footnote-4">4</a></li>
<li><strong>Peraton</strong>, the "prime integrator" running the $12.5 billion overhaul. Contract ceiling $1.5 billion.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-5" href="#footnote-5">5</a> FAA staff rated its finances "high risk." More on that in a minute.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-6" href="#footnote-6">6</a></li>
<li><strong>L3Harris</strong>, which has run the FAA's telecom network since 2002 and sent the technician to El Dorado. Duffy says he's "stuck with" them.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-7" href="#footnote-7">7</a> Hold that thought for part two.</li>
<li><strong>Markwayne Mullin</strong>, DHS Secretary, our resident superintelligence theorist.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-8" href="#footnote-8">8</a></li>
<li><strong>Sean Cairncross</strong>, National Cyber Director, the president's top cyber adviser. And that's not me throwing shade at his advisory skills ... about two hours ago a White House official let us know he's stepping down.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-9" href="#footnote-9">9</a> Nothing says leadership in a crisis like ... literally quitting.</li>
</ul>
<p>Before we all (hopefully) forget Sean ever existed, you should know his office was one of the government offices OpenAI briefed before the PHP bugs went public, and his former policy chief went to work at OpenAI in October.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-10" href="#footnote-10">10</a> Pentagon CIO Kirsten Davies is reportedly stepping in as acting director.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-11" href="#footnote-11">11</a> I'm sure it's fine. Patched systems are just "not coming back as expected in all cases."</p>
<h2>Not #1: "Not a cyberattack"</h2>
<p>Fine. Nobody's found one, and I'm not going to invent one. But listen to how the White House explained it instead. The outages were "separate incidents caused by companies patching vulnerabilities and upgrading outdated systems," and DNI Jay Clayton said: "As a result of SI and the ability to scan systems more quickly, the ability to identify vulnerabilities has increased." Patched systems are "not coming back as expected in all cases." His summary: "it's a good news story."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-12" href="#footnote-12">12</a></p>
<p>Secretary Rubio added: "Companies, which is a good thing, are now immediately sharing vulnerabilities because they don't want to be accused of covering up something."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-13" href="#footnote-13">13</a></p>
<p>Sharing with whom? OpenAI says that before it went public with the PHP bugs that sent everyone scrambling to patch this fall, it worked with "the White House's Super Intelligence Force," which Clayton leads, plus CISA and the National Cyber Director.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-14" href="#footnote-14">14</a> The president's AI order set up a federal clearinghouse to receive those bugs.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-15" href="#footnote-15">15</a> And on Oct. 30, the Transportation Secretary's own office bought a $1 million subscription to OpenAI's "Daybreak" security product.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-16" href="#footnote-16">16</a></p>
<p>So the official story is: our AI found bugs, our officials pushed the fixes, the fixes broke things, and that's good news. [P]</p>
<h2>Not #2: "Not SMART"</h2>
<p>The FAA's shiny AI traffic tool is called SMART, built by Air Space Intelligence under an $875 million contract. When the ground stop hit, the company was quick to say it was "not based on or a result of the SMART system."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-17" href="#footnote-17">17</a> (Raw Story, citing FEC records, reports $12,500 from Air Space Intelligence to the congressional campaign of Duffy's son-in-law. Small world!)<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-18" href="#footnote-18">18</a></p>
<p>And you know what? I believe them! It's not SMART. Never thought it was, Duffy.</p>
<p>Here's why. DOT's own letter to GAO, signed Aug. 12 by Assistant Secretary for Administration Dr. Anne Byrd, lists SMART as a <strong>Phase 2</strong> system still waiting on "approval and funding." So SMART isn't even in the phase that's under way.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-19" href="#footnote-19">19</a></p>
<p>But the same letter says the Phase 1 schedule "will be enhanced by the project Integrator Peraton's expertise and an AI-enabled Integrated Master Schedule (IMS) tool," and that "Peraton has developed an advanced IMS tool, using AI, that will coordinate and manage project scheduling across the BNATCS portfolio." Then: "These capabilities are still being fully optimized."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-20" href="#footnote-20">20</a></p>
<p>GAO's response: as of May, the tool "was undergoing testing, and the business rules for using the tool and the new process had not been established."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-21" href="#footnote-21">21</a></p>
<p>Peraton isn't shy about it either. "We're deploying revolutionary agentic AI for the Peraton team and the entire FAA enterprise," its executive said in April. "Schedules are stress tested before they break."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-22" href="#footnote-22">22</a> Peraton's own post that week listed the copper-to-fiber "Wires" work first and said: "Powering it all: Peraton's agentic AI analyzing 1.5 million records every single day."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-23" href="#footnote-23">23</a></p>
<p>So when the agency says "it wasn't SMART," it's telling the truth about the wrong AI. [P]</p>
<p>Oh, and the two FAA managers who objected that SMART was being launched "too hastily"? The New York Times reports both were removed from their positions.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-24" href="#footnote-24">24</a> So that's how dissent goes over.</p>
<h2>Not #3: "Not Peraton's program"</h2>
<p>Back to Bedford's line. Four government documents say otherwise.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>The job posting.</strong> The FAA's August 2025 request for proposals puts the integrator in charge of the "Integrated Master Schedule," asks bidders for "Transition Planning and Cutover Design," and lists the communications work as "updating FAA's network from Time Division Multiplexing (TDM) to a fully modernized Internet Protocol (IP)."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-25" href="#footnote-25">25</a> That's the El Dorado job, by name.</li>
<li><strong>GAO.</strong> Existing contracts "were modified to grant Peraton the authority to manage those contractors on FAA's behalf."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-26" href="#footnote-26">26</a> Those contractors include L3Harris.</li>
<li><strong>DOT's letter.</strong> Peraton's AI tool manages scheduling "across the BNATCS portfolio."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-27" href="#footnote-27">27</a></li>
<li><strong>The website.</strong> El Dorado, Phase 1, in progress.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-28" href="#footnote-28">28</a></li>
</ol>
<p>And my favorite, from Bedford himself in April: "Peraton, think of them as an extension of me."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-29" href="#footnote-29">29</a></p>
<h2>Not #4: "Not waived"</h2>
<p>This one's my favorite "not." In that Aug. 12 letter, DOT told GAO: "The Administrator did not waive BNATCS from the Acquisition Management System (AMS) and the requirement to develop a cost estimate."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-30" href="#footnote-30">30</a></p>
<p>GAO, which had read the actual waiver, quoted it back. Bedford's Aug. 28, 2025 memo is "a blanket waiver of the existing Acquisition Management System, including its contract clauses, policies, and guidance ... as well as financial, legal, technology, <strong>security</strong>, and program review processes, as necessary, for all activities associated with BNATCS."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-31" href="#footnote-31">31</a> The justification? The airspace's "critical role in national security and defense," so "swift and decisive action is essential."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-32" href="#footnote-32">32</a></p>
<p>Read that list again. <em>Security</em> review processes, waived, for everything tied to the overhaul. Including, presumably, the new routers and the AI that schedules when they go in. [P]</p>
<p>(Credit where due: The Air Current reported back in September 2025 that the memo lets the program skip FAA policy "without limitation."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-33" href="#footnote-33">33</a> The memo itself still isn't public. It's on my FOIA list.)</p>
<p>Meanwhile, the same August 2025 job posting required everything to "undergo thorough testing before initial operations."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-34" href="#footnote-34">34</a> Nice requirement. Shame about the waiver.</p>
<h2>Not #5: "Not Duffy's pick"</h2>
<p>The FAA says career staff ran the selection.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-35" href="#footnote-35">35</a> Here's what else we know:</p>
<ul>
<li>FAA staff told Duffy in October 2025 that Peraton's financial condition was "high risk" and its rival's was "low risk." The rival, Parsons, scored better on three of four technical factors. Peraton's answers "often focused on matters irrelevant to the discussion topic."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-36" href="#footnote-36">36</a></li>
<li>Bedford said the recommendation went to Duffy, then to the president, who "sharpened the pencil by demanding a two hundred million-dollar reduction in the fee cost."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-37" href="#footnote-37">37</a> Duffy's version, on a podcast: Trump called Peraton's CEO, and "The CEO called back in eight minutes and said he would do it for us for $1.5 billion."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-38" href="#footnote-38">38</a></li>
<li>Peraton's lobbyist was Duffy's former spokesman, who also sits on the board of the nonprofit that paid for Duffy's family road-trip show.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-39" href="#footnote-39">39</a></li>
<li>After the award, FAA contract changes removed "performance bond optional pricing" from one Peraton order and "IRB requirements" from the master contract.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-40" href="#footnote-40">40</a></li>
</ul>
<p>None of that proves anything about El Dorado. It does tell you who was holding the clipboard. [P]</p>
<h2>Trump speed</h2>
<p>The FAA's own dashboard brags that "we're moving at Trump speed."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-41" href="#footnote-41">41</a> In October it hit exactly its plan: 150 copper-to-fiber cutovers in one month, about five a day.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-42" href="#footnote-42">42</a> GAO counted 2,560 done in May. The dashboard says 3,651 as of Nov. 16.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-43" href="#footnote-43">43</a></p>
<p>GAO warned in September what rushing an unintegrated schedule could do: "more system outages, ground stops, flight delays, and safety incidents."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-44" href="#footnote-44">44</a> Two months later, the El Dorado job ran on a Friday afternoon "in order to meet the project's ambitious schedule."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-45" href="#footnote-45">45</a> Duffy's own account: "we brought in our routers and did the work, and that's when we saw the flooding of the network."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-46" href="#footnote-46">46</a> Juniper's parent company says "our equipment was working as configured."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-47" href="#footnote-47">47</a></p>
<p>Rep. James Walkinshaw put it plainly: they did it "on a busy travel day, as opposed to in the middle of the night ... because they're rushing."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-48" href="#footnote-48">48</a></p>
<h2>Who's checking? (Nobody. It's nobody.)</h2>
<ul>
<li>AI helped <strong>grade</strong> the bids. The FAA's job posting said it "may use a proprietary AI model such as Amazon Nova Pro in Bedrock," providing "automated analysis and scoring recommendations." The proposals were to be "deleted after the review is completed."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-49" href="#footnote-49">49</a></li>
<li>AI <strong>schedules</strong> the work.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-50" href="#footnote-50">50</a></li>
<li><strong>Security review</strong> was waived by memo.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-51" href="#footnote-51">51</a></li>
<li>The nominee to run CISA, the agency for cyber incidents, said of Jan. 6: "I don't believe it was an attack ... I know what I saw on the news." He wouldn't say who won 2020. Sens. Tillis (R) and Wyden (D) are both placing holds.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-52" href="#footnote-52">52</a></li>
<li>The nominee to run GAO, the office that caught all this, is Russ Vought's deputy at OMB. His pitch: "I'm not here as a plant, a stooge, a lackey for anybody."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-53" href="#footnote-53">53</a> I'll give him this much: at least he didn't say he saw it on the news.</li>
<li>Not the president. He spent the afternoon pardoning Pete Rose.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-54" href="#footnote-54">54</a> (I just wanted to work that in.)</li>
</ul>
<p>And the president, from China, already has the ending written: "If anybody did that to us and we found out about it, they know what the repercussions would be with me."<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-55" href="#footnote-55">55</a></p>
<h2>Coming in part two: the network Duffy's "stuck with"</h2>
<p>L3Harris runs the network. It also owned Trenchant, a unit that built hacking tools for the U.S. government and its allies. Trenchant's former general manager pleaded guilty to stealing some of those tools, which prosecutors say he sold to a Russian broker.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-56" href="#footnote-56">56</a> Former employees told TechCrunch that an iPhone hacking kit called "Coruna" was "at least in part" developed by Trenchant.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-57" href="#footnote-57">57</a> Coruna turned up again this month, in malware planted on 178 big-brand websites, some of them federal vendors.<a class="footnote-anchor" data-component-name="FootnoteAnchorToDOM" id="footnote-anchor-58" href="#footnote-58">58</a> And the NSA told the whole government to patch PHP "immediately" in October, then had its own patch take down its network in November.</p>
