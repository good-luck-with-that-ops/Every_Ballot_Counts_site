<h2>Act 3: The pipe is already built (and it did the paperwork)</h2>
<p>Here's the part that got me. Everyone's waiting for a secret master database. They don't need a secret one. They published one.</p>
<p>PRAC was created in 2020 to watch COVID money. Its data system is huge: "over 1 billion records" from "more than five dozen public, non-public, and commercial data sources," with Social Security number checks and "authority to store and leverage law-enforcement sensitive data" (<a href="https://oversight.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Dieffenbach-PRAC-Written-Testimony.pdf">PRAC testimony</a>). [D]</p>
<p>Then the One Big Beautiful Bill extended PRAC to 2034, gave it $88 million, and expanded its reach to "all funds provided in the OBBB." [D] And in January, CIGIE rewrote PRAC's Privacy Act notice to match (<a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-01-30/pdf/2026-01843.pdf">FR 2026-01843</a>). [D] Read these slowly:</p>
<ul><li><p>It changes "all references to 'Coronavirus Funds' to 'covered funds.'" So the COVID watchdog's database is now the everything-bill watchdog's database. [D]</p></li><li><p>Who's in it: "those who have applied for, sought, or received Federal funds." That's a lot of us. [D]</p></li><li><p>Where data comes from: "Publicly and/or commercially available data sets," federal agencies, and states. [D]</p></li><li><p>Who it can go to: "the appropriate Federal, state, local, tribal, or foreign agency" for any "potential violation of civil or criminal law." [D] (Yes, that one dates to 2021. It just used to be about COVID money.)</p></li><li><p><strong>New:</strong> it can go to Treasury's Do Not Pay system to stop "improper payments to an applicant for, or recipient of, Federal funds, including funds disbursed by a state ... in a state-administered, federally funded program." [D]</p></li></ul>
<p><img src="https://everyballotcounts.io/assets/img/poi/posts/fruit_quote_square_1080.png" alt="The PRAC notice, Federal Register, Jan. 30, 2026: all references to Coronavirus Funds changed to covered funds"></p>
<p>Who signed it? CIGIE's executive director, on Jan. 27, 2026. [D] Nobody took that job to fight this.</p>
<p>Now follow the pipe. In August, PRAC joined DOJ's new National Fraud Detection Center, alongside HSI, the FBI, IRS criminal investigators, about a dozen inspectors general and the secretaries of state of seven states (<a href="https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-announces-launch-national-fraud-detection-center">DOJ</a>; <a href="https://www.fbi.gov/news/stories/new-center-brings-agencies-together-to-detect-fraud-across-government-programs">FBI</a>). [D] Seven secretaries of state. In a fraud center. With immigration agents. What exactly are they contributing? Nobody's said. [D]</p>
<p>And the cherry on top: under the Inspector General Act, a data match "performed by an Inspector General or by an agency in coordination with an Inspector General" isn't a "matching program" at all (<a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/406">5 U.S.C. 406(j)</a>). [D] CIGIE's own guidance says agencies "can therefore engage in matching without following the [matching law's] requirements if the matches are conducted in coordination with an OIG" (<a href="https://www.ignet.gov/sites/default/files/files/CMA%20Exemption%20Overview%20Guidance%20(June%202017">CIGIE</a>.pdf)). [D] No matching agreement, no data integrity board, no public notice. Just put an IG in the room. Good thing they've been picking the IGs. [P]</p>
<p>Brookings put it better than I can: the Privacy Act may not stop "a single centralized government database integrating agency records if the government chooses to follow the act's established procedures" (<a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/privacy-under-siege-doges-one-big-beautiful-database/">Brookings</a>). [R] Would every piece of this survive a lawsuit? Maybe not. But a lawful-looking pipe is all you need on the day someone declares an incident and says the data has to be pulled together to check it. [P]</p>
<p>So: China memo, incident declared, FBI takes the evidence, and now there's a "need" to run every federally held record through the fraud pipe to find the "tainted" ones. That's your master database, with a Federal Register citation. [P]</p>
